Three key systems

Symmetric, asymmetric, and a PIN

EMV runs two completely separate cryptosystems at once, for two different threats.

01

Symmetric — proves the card is present

A three‑step key ladder ends in a MAC over the transaction data: the ARQC. Only a chip holding the right key can produce it, and it changes every single time because the counter moves.

IMKAC → MKcard = f(IMK, PAN‖PSN) → SK = f(MK, ATC) → ARQC = MACSK(data)
02

Asymmetric — proves it offline, instantly

The terminal can't call anyone mid‑transaction on a plane or a bus. So it verifies an RSA certificate chain rooted in a scheme CA key baked into the terminal, then challenges the card to sign a fresh random number.

Scheme CA PK → Issuer PK cert (90) → ICC PK cert (9F46) → SDAD (9F4B)
03

The PIN — proves it's you, not the card

Offline PIN is enciphered with the card's own public key and checked by the chip; the terminal never learns it. Online PIN instead travels as a PIN block under acquirer working keys, re‑enciphered at every HSM hop.

PIN block ‖ random ‖ ICC data → RSAICC‑PK → VERIFY (00 20 00 88)

Scope of the guarantee

What a cryptogram
does and doesn't prove

✓ Proven
  • The real chip was there. Only it holds MKAC, and it never leaves the die.
  • This exact transaction. Amount, currency, date, terminal country and the terminal's random number are all inside the MAC.
  • Freshness. The ATC increments once per transaction; a replayed ARQC arrives with a stale counter and is rejected.
  • The issuer answered. The ARPC comes back and the chip itself verifies it — a fake "approved" from the network doesn't fool the card.
✗ Not proven
  • That your card number is secret. The PAN and expiry are read in the clear — which is why skimmed data still fuels card‑not‑present fraud.
  • That the cardholder consented, unless a CVM ran. "No CVM" contactless relies on limits, not proof.
  • That the terminal is honest. A crooked terminal can lie about its own risk checks; the issuer sees the TVR but can't audit the shop.
  • Distance. A relay attack forwards genuine APDUs over a network in real time — which is why timing bounds exist in newer contactless kernels.

The acronyms

ARQCAuthorisation Request Cryptogram — the card's MAC asking to go online.
TCTransaction Certificate — the cryptogram of an approved transaction, kept for clearing.
AACApplication Authentication Cryptogram — the cryptogram of a decline.
ARPCAuthorisation Response Cryptogram — the issuer's signed reply, checked by the chip.
ATCApplication Transaction Counter — increments every transaction; kills replay.
CDACombined Data Authentication — the card RSA‑signs the cryptogram and the transaction data together.
TVRTerminal Verification Results — 5 bytes of "what went wrong" flags.
CDOLCard Risk Management Data Object List — the card telling the terminal exactly what to feed it.
IADIssuer Application Data — the card's private note to its issuer, including the CVR.
