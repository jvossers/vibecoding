Trio covers A levels in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. It can't know what you enjoy or are good at, and your grades matter more than your combination, so talk it through with your school or college careers adviser too.

How the scores work

Every number is relative: 100 means the best combination of three in Trio's data for that measure.

  • University access: how many of the listed university courses your subjects meet the requirements for. Missing a required subject rules a course out. Missing a recommended one counts as partly open. The most selective courses count for more.
  • Career prospects: which degree areas are open to you, weighted by how graduates in that area typically do (earnings and employment a few years after graduating) and how many students study it.
  • Keeps options open: how many degree areas stay open, whatever they lead to.
  • Fits together: of the degrees your subjects point towards, how many you fully qualify for. Subjects that support each other score higher than three unrelated ones.
  • Fit for your goal: if you choose up to three things you'd like to study, how well the combination meets what those degrees ask for.
  • Overall: a blend of all of these. Prioritise changes the mix of the first three. With goals set, goal fit is 40% of the overall score.

With fewer than three subjects, scores show the best you can still reach (“up to”).

Suggestions are ranked by overall score. They leave out subjects you've marked as not offered at your school.
