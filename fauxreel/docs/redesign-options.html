ChatReel: eight redesign directions

Archived. These were presented when the app was still called ChatReel. Option C (Bubblegum) was chosen and built. The mockups are static HTML; the phone image is an embedded screenshot.

Each option restyles the app around the phone preview: colours, type, controls and the export button. The rendered chat stays exactly as it is. Mockups are shown at phone width, which is how most people will use it.

OPTION A

Darkroom: a video editor in your pocket

Treats ChatReel as a tiny editing suite. It's warm charcoal instead of cold blue-black, uses an amber playhead and a red recording light, and shows times in monospaced "timecode". The closest to what you have now, with much more character.

Controls

  • The scrubber becomes a timeline: each message is a clip (green = theirs, amber = yours) with a glowing playhead. You can see and jump to every message.

Good

  • Timeline is a genuinely useful new control
  • Least work to get to from today

Watch out

  • Still a dark tool UI. Less fun than the chats it makes.
OPTION B

Tabloid: gossip-magazine energy

These chats are basically screenshots people share for drama, so dress the app like a tabloid. There's cream newsprint with a dot screen, a big italic serif masthead, thick black rules and one hot tomato accent. The controls are chunky outlined chips with hard offset shadows.

OPTION C

Bubblegum: the UI is made of chat bubbles

Soft pink, candy colours and chunky rounded type. Every control borrows the shape of a message bubble, with one tail corner. Selected tabs flip their tail like a "sent" message, and buttons press down with a solid bottom shadow, like toy keys.

OPTION D

Swiss Grid: calm, exact, grown-up

Pure white and black hairlines on a strict grid, with numbered sections and one signal-green accent. The preview shares a split panel with big stats. Nothing is decorative. It feels like a well-made instrument.

OPTION E

Aqua Glass: 2006 phone nostalgia

Glossy, bubbly "Frutiger Aero": a sky-to-grass gradient, frosted glass panels, jelly buttons with a shine on top and a green gel progress bar. It's a nod to the era when chat apps felt new.

OPTION F

Retro OS: ChatReel.exe

The whole app is a Windows 95 program on a teal desktop. It has bevelled grey buttons, navy title bars, radio buttons in group boxes and a chunky block progress bar. It's a funny contrast with the modern phone inside.

OPTION G

Riso Print: two-ink poster

Looks like a risograph print from an art-school zine. There are only two "inks" (fluoro pink and deep blue, plus a teal) that overlap and darken where they cross, slightly off-register, on grainy paper. Arty and bold.

OPTION H

Notebook: scribbled on lined paper

A school notebook, with a red margin line, blue rules, handwriting, highlighter and tape. The preview is a taped-in polaroid, choices are ticked boxes, and the export button is a sticky note. Cosy and personal.

You can also mix and match, e.g. B's chips on A's colours, E's glossy buttons on D's grid, or C's per-message progress bar in any option. The timeline (A) and per-message progress (C) are behaviour changes worth considering whichever look wins.
