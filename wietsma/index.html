02 — THE CORE THESIS

Don't pay for reach he gets for free

With €250–500/month against a market dominated by free organic content, the worst thing he can do is buy broad awareness. That's already handled. Paid budget should do the one job organic does worst: converting intent into sales, on demand, at the bottom of the funnel.

01

Capture, don't create

The channel already creates demand. Paid ads should intercept students who are already searching for exam help and hand them the bundle — the shortest possible path from intent to €35.

02

Concentrate, don't sprinkle

€350/mo split four ways learns nothing. Put the money where intent is highest (Search), prove a profitable cost-per-sale, then expand. One channel mastered beats four channels guessed.

03

Spend when they buy

Demand is seasonal, so the budget should be too. Pour spend into the exam and herkansing windows; go near-dark in the quiet months. A flat monthly spend wastes the off-season.

03 — WHERE PAID FITS

Organic fills the top. Paid closes the bottom.

His YouTube channel is an enormous, free top-of-funnel machine. The leak is at the bottom — turning warm viewers into buyers. That's precisely where a tiny budget has outsized leverage.

AWARENESS · handled by organic

Hundreds of thousands of students already know the brand from YouTube.

Paid role: minimal. Maybe light TikTok/Reels later to seed new cohorts each school year.

CONSIDERATION · retarget the warm

Viewers who watched videos or visited the site but didn't buy.

Paid role: cheap retargeting on Meta/YouTube reminding them the bundle exists before exams.

INTENT & PURCHASE · paid's main job

Students Googling "natuurkunde examen oefenen" or "samenvatting natuurkunde vwo" with wallet in hand.

Paid role: primary spend here. Search ads + brand defense catch them at the decision moment.

04 — CHANNEL PRIORITY

The ranked playbook

Ordered by how directly each channel turns a euro into a €35 sale at this budget. Start at the top. Only move down once the channel above is profitable and you've spare budget.

1

Google Search Highest intent Start here now

People typing exam queries into Google are the warmest buyers on the internet. With a niche subject, clicks are cheap and conversion is high. This is the single best home for the first €100–200/month.

  • Brand defense: bid on "Meneer Wietsma" so competitors/publishers can't intercept his own traffic — pennies per click.
  • Non-brand intent: target subject + exam keywords, sent straight to the €35 bundle page.
  • Start with exact/phrase match, a tight budget cap, and a single tightly-themed campaign.

07 — FOUNDATIONS

Two gaps to close before scaling

Paid traffic without measurement is gambling. He already has the two hardest assets — a sales page and a free-content funnel. Two pieces are missing, and both are quick wins.

✓ Already have

A sales landing page

The destination exists — paid traffic has somewhere to convert. Worth a quick conversion-focused tidy: clear price, social proof from YouTube, single obvious buy button.

✓ Already have

Free YouTube → website funnel

Videos already drive viewers to the site. This is gold for retargeting once a pixel is installed — a warm, free audience to re-engage cheaply.

✕ Missing — fix first

Conversion tracking

Without it you can't tell which ads make sales. Non-negotiable before spending.

  • Install Google Ads conversion tracking + GA4 on the purchase confirmation.
  • Add the Meta Pixel for retargeting + lookalikes.
  • ~1–2 hours of setup; everything downstream depends on it.
✕ Missing — high leverage

Email capture

Most visitors won't buy on the first visit. An email list lets you re-sell for free, every exam season.

  • Offer a free sample (one exam topic / a summary PDF) for an email.
  • Nurture toward the €35 bundle as exams approach — owned, repeatable, zero ad cost.
08 — MESSAGING

Two audiences, two angles

Students respond to relief from stress and trust in the face they already know. Parents respond to outcomes and reassurance. Same product, deliberately different words.

For students · TikTok / IG / YouTube

"You already watch me. Now ace it."

"Examen natuurkunde over 3 weken? Ik heb alles wat je moet weten in één bundel. €35. Geen stress."

Lean on the parasocial trust he's built. Casual, his face, his voice, the exact tone of his free videos. The ad shouldn't feel like an ad — it should feel like one more Meneer Wietsma clip that happens to end in a link.

For parents · Facebook / Search

"The teacher their class already trusts."

"Helpt jouw kind zich zeker te voelen bij het natuurkunde-examen — uitleg van de docent die honderdduizenden leerlingen al volgen."

Outcome and reassurance, not slang. Mention the brand's scale as proof ("the channel students already watch"), name the price as affordable peace of mind, and make buying-for-someone-else effortless.

09 — THE PLAN

A 90-day rollout

Crawl, walk, run. Prove profitability small before committing real money to the spring peak.

  4. Only now consider a paid TikTok test with proven creative.

11 — GUARDRAILS

What not to do

  • Don't buy broad awareness. Organic already owns the top of the funnel — paying for reach is paying for what's free.
  • Don't spread €350 across four channels at once. You'll learn nothing. Master Search first.
  • Don't spend flat all year. The off-season burns budget. Concentrate on the exam peaks.
  • Don't run ads before tracking works. Untracked spend is a guess, not a strategy.
  • Don't outsource his voice. The brand's power is him — generic agency creative will underperform his own clips.
  • Don't chase TikTok ad ROI early. A €35 one-off rarely pays back from cold TikTok ads at this budget.

He spent years earning the trust of a generation of students.
Paid advertising's only job is to be there the moment that trust turns into a purchase.
